SECTION I — SYNOPSIS [span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)

1.0 Introduction [span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span)

The design of modern aerospace vehicle concepts increasingly requires the detailed understanding and synergistic use of ever more complex fluid phenomena[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span). Both industry and government view Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) as a critical, potentially efficient and cost-effective technology for the development of advanced aerospace configurations[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span). The overall technical problem in CFD is to devise reliable numerical approaches to simulate the complex fluid physics arising in flow about complex shapes/topologies[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span). Any approach to the solution of this problem must address the following issues: (a) appropriate modeling of the physics, (b) proper match between physics and numerics, (c) accuracy, (d) geometric flexibility, and (e) ease of use[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span). In today’s environment, these issues must also be treated in ways which are compatible and synergistic with both proven and emerging high-performance computer architectures[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span). The major thrust of the Science Center’s CFD program is to effect rapid but sustained strides in algorithm and code development to exploit the increasing power of supercomputers, super-minicomputers, parallel computers, and graphics workstations in order to systematically and continually improve the use of CFD in state-of-the-art design and analysis scenarios[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span).

In FY 90, we embarked on an ambitious plan to begin the development of the next generation of CFD codes and technology — the successors to the proven and successful USA-series (Unified Solution Algorithms) of codes that are currently in widespread use in Rockwell and in Government agencies[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span). The new generation is based on very significant developments in computational algorithms accomplished in past and present research and will also benefit from the considerable experience the CFD Department’s researchers have acquired in working with more than one hundred users throughout Rockwell and in other DoD agencies[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span).

In this endeavor, we looked forward to participate in Government-sponsored research programs which are particularly well matched with our quest for the next higher plateau in CFD capability[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span). The current contract (awarded September, 1990 by NWC to Rockwell International Science Center) fits that category extremely well[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span). The general objective of this program is to conduct an algorithm research and development effort that will result in the demonstrated capability to provide accurate numerical flowfield data for situations typical of weapon carriage and release from an internal weapon bay[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span). This program permits us to apply and specialize the very general algorithmic capability that we have developed to the challenging problem of computing the airloads and separation characteristics of internally carried weapons[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span). It will serve as a key demonstration of the flexibility of the new capability that we have built[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span). In turn, we hope to provide NWC with a very powerful new CFD capability that will serve them well for years to come[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span). During the first phase of the program, the goal is to demonstrate inviscid 3-D static and 2-D dynamic capability[span_38](start_span)[span_38](end_span). In the next phase, the focus will be on viscous static 3-D and inviscid dynamic 3-D capability[span_39](start_span)[span_39](end_span). The third phase deals with 3-D viscous and dynamic CFD analysis of a missile emerging from a weapon bay for which the missile trajectory is determined by integrating the CFD flowfield solver with a six degree-of-freedom trajectory algorithm[span_40](start_span)[span_40](end_span). This report presents work done during Phase I along with detailed reference material for the overall computational framework[span_41](start_span)[span_41](end_span).

2.0 Summary [span_42](start_span)[span_42](end_span)

During Phase I, the focus was on inviscid flows (2-D dynamic and 3-D static)[span_43](start_span)[span_43](end_span). A summary of progress for Phase I follows[span_44](start_span)[span_44](end_span):

  1. Three versions of a UNIVERSE-series flow solver were delivered to NWC[span_45](start_span)[span_45](end_span). The first is UNIV1 and is written entirely in Fortran[span_46](start_span)[span_46](end_span). The second is UNIVCF and includes sections in C which can be linked with the Fortran routines in UNIV1[span_47](start_span)[span_47](end_span). The third is UNIVC which is almost totally written in the C language[span_48](start_span)[span_48](end_span). It is expected that UNIVC will be the version to grow into Phase II[span_49](start_span)[span_49](end_span).
  2. A 2-D unstructured grid generation software (TRIM2D) has been delivered to NWC[span_50](start_span)[span_50](end_span).
  3. A 3-D unstructured grid generation software (TRIM3D) has been delivered to NWC[span_51](start_span)[span_51](end_span).
  4. The UNIVC flow solver includes a built-in 2-D plotting capability that can display results on Tektronix terminals[span_52](start_span)[span_52](end_span). A pilot version of 3-D visualization software running on Silicon Graphics workstations has also been delivered[span_53](start_span)[span_53](end_span).
  5. A 3-D static inviscid CFD flow field computation around an F-18 configuration with pylon, rack, and bombs has been demonstrated[span_54](start_span)[span_54](end_span).
  6. The inviscid flowfield for a 3-D missile in a 3-D cavity has been completed[span_55](start_span)[span_55](end_span).
  7. A store-tracking approach exploiting local grid adaptation has been developed[span_56](start_span)[span_56](end_span).
  8. A 2-D case of a weapon in a bomb bay has been computed as a static case and the dynamic computation is in progress[span_57](start_span)[span_57](end_span).
  9. Multiple unit problems were solved and documented in Section 14[span_58](start_span)[span_58](end_span).
  10. Manuals and version description documents were delivered for UNIVC, TRIM3D, and TRIM2D[span_59](start_span)[span_59](end_span).

3.0 Conclusions [span_60](start_span)[span_60](end_span)

Section II shows that a new CFD framework has been developed which is more accurate and versatile than the previous state of the art for solving fluid dynamics problems involving complex geometries and complex flow phenomena such as shock waves[span_61](start_span)[span_61](end_span). Key aspects include[span_62](start_span)[span_62](end_span):

  • Automatic tetrahedral mesh generation for complex geometries (Space Shuttle, F-18, missile cavity)[span_63](start_span)[span_63](end_span).
  • Flexible spatial accuracy up to 6th order in 1-D and up to 4th order in 2-D and 3-D[span_64](start_span)[span_64](end_span).
  • Higher-order shape functions for accurate geometry representation matching solution order[span_65](start_span)[span_65](end_span).
  • Implementation of unrestricted Essentially Non-Oscillatory (ENO) interpolation schemes[span_66](start_span)[span_66](end_span).
  • Inclusion of exact and approximate Riemann Solvers (Godunov, Roe, Osher, Rusanov, Harten-Lax)[span_67](start_span)[span_67](end_span).
  • Unified solver capability for 1-D, 2-D, axisymmetric, and 3-D formulations within a single code architecture[span_68](start_span)[span_68](end_span).
  • Support for hexahedral, triangular prism, and tetrahedral conservation cells[span_69](start_span)[span_69](end_span).

SECTION II — TECHNICAL DISCUSSION [span_70](start_span)[span_70](end_span)

4.0 Numerical Framework for UNIVERSE-Series Codes [span_71](start_span)[span_71](end_span)

UNIVERSE denotes a Unification of Essentially Non-oscillatory Interpolation techniques with a geometrically VERSatile implementation[span_72](start_span)[span_72](end_span). The ENO interpolation facilitates the construction of numerical algorithms which can "capture" discontinuities such as shock waves and which are of arbitrarily high orders of accuracy[span_73](start_span)[span_73](end_span). UNIVERSE codes implement a truly multidimensional unrestricted version of ENO schemes that work with arbitrary cell shapes[span_74](start_span)[span_74](end_span).

Integral Form of Conservation Laws [span_75](start_span)[span_75](end_span)

The system of conservation laws is represented by[span_76](start_span)[span_76](end_span):

$$\frac{\partial q}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial z} = 0$$ [span_77](start_span)[span_77](end_span)

Applying the Gauss divergence theorem over a volume $V$ with surface $S$ yields the integral form[span_78](start_span)[span_78](end_span):

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\bar{q}V\right) + \iint_{S} \left(\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n}\right) dS = 0$$ [span_79](start_span)[span_79](end_span)

where $\bar{q}$ is the cell average value of the dependent variables, $\mathbf{F} = f_1 \mathbf{i} + f_2 \mathbf{j} + f_3 \mathbf{k}$, and $\mathbf{n}$ is the outward unit normal[span_80](start_span)[span_80](end_span).

5.0 Constitutive Equations [span_81](start_span)[span_81](end_span)

The code framework supports multiple constitutive model systems[span_82](start_span)[span_82](end_span):

  • Linear Wave Equation: $f_1 = aq, f_2 = bq, f_3 = cq$[span_83](start_span)[span_83](end_span).
  • Inviscid Burgers' Equation: $f_1 = aq^2/2, f_2 = bq^2/2, f_3 = cq^2/2$[span_84](start_span)[span_84](end_span).
  • Euler & Navier-Stokes Equations: Governing mass, momentum, and energy conservation for perfect gas flow[span_85](start_span)[span_85](end_span).

6.0 Riemann Solvers [span_86](start_span)[span_86](end_span)

At every cell interface quadrature point, a local 1-D Riemann problem is solved along the cell boundary normal direction[span_87](start_span)[span_87](end_span). The solution provides exact or approximate flux evaluation ($F_R$) using left ($q_l$) and right ($q_r$) reconstructed states[span_88](start_span)[span_88](end_span). Built-in solvers include Godunov (exact), Roe (approximate linear), Osher (rarefaction-based), Rusanov, and Lax-Friedrichs schemes[span_89](start_span)[span_89](end_span).

7.0 Geometry Formulation Details [span_90](start_span)[span_90](end_span)

Geometry transformations maps local coordinates $(\xi, \eta, \zeta)$ to physical space $(x, y, z)$ via shape functions $N_i(\xi, \eta, \zeta)$[span_91](start_span)[span_91](end_span). Integrations over cell faces use 4-point Gaussian quadrature formulas for high spatial accuracy[span_92](start_span)[span_92](end_span).

8.0 Topology Treatment [span_93](start_span)[span_93](end_span)

Unstructured connectivity maintains data structures for node-aligned and surface-aligned cells[span_94](start_span)[span_94](end_span). Local cell division and link-removal procedures allow dynamic mesh editing when tracking moving objects[span_95](start_span)[span_95](end_span).

9.0 Grid Generation Methodology [span_96](start_span)[span_96](end_span)

Two automated grid generators are integrated[span_97](start_span)[span_97](end_span):

  • TRIM2D: 2-D Delaunay triangulation generator using an advancing front technique[span_98](start_span)[span_98](end_span).
  • TRIM3D: 3-D advancing-front generator utilizing binary tree (heap-sort) face tracking and octree spatial data structures for efficient 3-D tetrahedral mesh creation[span_99](start_span)[span_99](end_span).

10.0 Store Tracking and Grid Adaptation [span_100](start_span)[span_100](end_span)

Dynamic store separation is modeled by integrating unsteady fluid flow solver outputs with 6-DOF dynamic trajectory equations[span_101](start_span)[span_101](end_span). Mesh movement uses spring-analogy smoothing combined with localized cell division/addition and link removal[span_102](start_span)[span_102](end_span).

11.0 Boundary Condition Procedures [span_103](start_span)[span_103](end_span)

Boundary conditions are applied by setting local characteristic conditions or ghost-cell values mapped to boundary surface patch nodes[span_104](start_span)[span_104](end_span).

12.0 Software Architectural Issues [span_105](start_span)[span_105](end_span)

UNIVC is constructed in ANSI C to leverage dynamic memory allocation, flexible data structures, clean modularity, recursive search structures, and portable sockets/RPC graphics integration across UNIX supercomputers and graphics workstations[span_106](start_span)[span_106](end_span).

13.0 Discussion of Options and Features [span_107](start_span)[span_107](end_span)

Features include selectable spatial/temporal accuracy order, optional saving of least-squares polynomial constructors, flux masks for dimensional reduction, and built-in 2-D/3-D plotting drivers[span_108](start_span)[span_108](end_span).

14.0 Illustrative Examples [span_109](start_span)[span_109](end_span)

Validation cases documented in the report include 1-D linear/nonlinear wave propagation, Ringleb flow accuracy studies, subsonic/hypersonic cylinder flows, axisymmetric sphere-cylinder flow, 2-D store release, full 3-D F-18 aircraft with store pylons, and 3-D Space Shuttle Orbiter/ET/SRB multibody flow simulations[span_110](start_span)[span_110](end_span).
